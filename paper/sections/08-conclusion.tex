\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We addressed the attribution of network connections to AI-agent executions and its use for network management. agentnet fuses kernel signals with a runtime-provided identity while reporting ambiguity, binds the identity to tasks so that it survives asynchronous interleaving in a shared MCP server, and drives live per-execution accounting and an in-kernel egress policy with the same identity. An evaluation against an independent destination-based reference shows that fusion turns faulty labels into reported ambiguity where the process structure is informative, that the task-scoped binding removes the misattributions of thread-keyed markers for asynchronous tools, and that the policy and accounting remain correct with concurrent LLM agents. The cost is dominated by exporting one record per connection: microseconds, against tool calls of milliseconds and model steps of seconds.
